# Discovery and prioritization

Use for weekly/monthly choices, new exam or language proposals, or a feature request whose learner benefit is unclear. Start with the resumed backlog and current evidence. Outages, blocked learning and verified answer errors retain priority over growth scores.

## Frame the learner problem

Describe who is affected, the exam/version/language, the situation, the learning task and the observed obstacle. Separate the requested feature from the underlying need. For example, a request for more questions might reflect missing syllabus coverage, weak explanations or difficulty finding existing practice; inspect the evidence before choosing a remedy.

Use current reports, feedback and existing journeys. Keep raw feedback, source IDs and business figures in private state. Record what contradicts the proposed explanation, not just supporting evidence. Country of access alone does not establish preferred language or demand for a local qualification. Do not invent interviews, learner quotes or research counts. Preparing interview questions does not authorize contacting learners.

## Connect outcome, opportunity and action

For a meaningful product choice, make a compact chain in the existing work record:

- **Outcome:** one learner outcome with its metric definition and known baseline, or `unmeasured` plus the missing measurement.
- **Opportunity:** the evidenced unmet need; keep unverified needs labeled as hypotheses.
- **Alternatives:** compare materially different ways to meet the need, including reuse/improvement of existing content or navigation. Include leaving the current behavior in place when that is a credible option. For a nontrivial choice, two or three alternatives are usually enough.
- **Assumption:** what must be true for the preferred alternative to help? Identify the consequential uncertainty in value, usability, feasibility or operating cost.
- **Probe:** the cheapest authorized observation, local prototype or small content pilot that could disprove that assumption. Define the evidence that would change the decision.

Do not turn an obvious correction into a discovery workshop. Search existing content and prior experiments before proposing new pages, features or infrastructure. Prefer one exam/language pilot over a portfolio-wide rollout when demand is uncertain.

## Choose work without false precision

Compare feasible candidates by learner harm/benefit, affected audience, confidence, effort, review/update cost and dependencies. Use qualitative high/medium/low with reasons when inputs are weak. Do not convert missing evidence into a low or zero score.

Use RICE only when the inputs are defensible: `reach × impact × confidence / effort`. Define one common reach period, one impact scale, confidence as a fraction and one effort unit including review and maintenance. Keep observed inputs distinct from estimates; check whether plausible estimate changes reverse the ranking. Scores inform judgment and never override correctness, privacy, existing commitments or release gates. A high-impact but uncertain assumption is a reason to test it, not to fabricate confidence.

## Decision brief and outcome roadmap

Record the following compactly in the existing journal/change record before implementation:

| Field | Decision content |
| --- | --- |
| Problem and evidence | Learner task, obstacle, evidence date, contradiction or gap |
| Choice and alternatives | Smallest complete intervention, why selected, what stays out of scope |
| Learning and acceptance | Riskiest assumption, observable acceptance, primary metric/denominator, guardrails |
| Delivery | Owner role, dependencies, local verification, release boundary and recovery |
| Follow-up | Evaluation window or evidence trigger; continue, revise, stop or inconclusive criteria |

For monthly planning, group the existing backlog into Now / Next / Later by outcome and dependency. Now should have actionable evidence and capacity; Next names the evidence or prerequisite that will make it ready; Later remains a candidate, not a release promise. Preserve already agreed dates and decisions unless new evidence justifies revisiting them.
