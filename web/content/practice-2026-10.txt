# Basic Food Safety (1-8)
Which example is a biological food safety hazard?|Norovirus on a ready-to-eat sandwich|A piece of plastic in a sandwich|Cleaning solution spilled on a sandwich|A metal fragment in a sandwich|A biological hazard is a harmful organism or an agent such as a virus. Norovirus can spread to ready-to-eat food through contaminated hands; plastic and metal are physical hazards, and cleaning solution is a chemical hazard.
A spray bottle of degreaser leaks onto uncovered dinner rolls. What type of contamination has occurred?|Chemical contamination|Physical contamination from packaging|Allergen cross-contact|Time-temperature abuse|Degreaser contains chemicals that must never contact food. Remove the affected rolls from service and tell the person in charge; wiping or reheating the rolls does not make them safe.
A food handler finds a small shard of glass in a bowl of soup. Which type of hazard is present?|Physical hazard|Biological hazard|Food allergen|Temperature-control failure|Glass is a foreign object that can injure a customer. Stop serving the affected food and report the problem so the person in charge can address the source and any other exposed food.
Which customer is generally at greater risk of becoming seriously ill from contaminated food?|An older adult living in a nursing home|A healthy college student|A healthy middle-aged delivery driver|A healthy adult restaurant employee|Older adults can have weaker immune defenses and are more vulnerable to foodborne illness. Young children and people with weakened immune systems are also at increased risk.
Which food normally requires time and temperature control for safety after preparation?|Cooked rice|Dry uncooked rice|Unopened dry crackers|Granulated sugar|Cooked rice can support pathogen growth and requires time and temperature control for safety, or TCS. Store it under proper temperature control and cool it correctly when saving it for later service.
A container of prepared food may have been contaminated, but it looks and smells normal. What should the food handler do?|Keep it from being served and notify the person in charge|Taste a small spoonful to check its safety|Serve it if no discoloration is visible|Mix it with a fresh batch to dilute the problem|Food can contain harmful pathogens without a noticeable change in appearance, smell, or taste. Do not taste suspect food; isolate it from service and follow the person in charge's disposal or other approved instructions.
Which canned item should a food handler reject during receiving?|A can with a swollen lid and leaking seam|A clean can with an intact seam and a readable label|An intact can delivered by an approved supplier|An undamaged can with the correct product label|Swelling and leakage can indicate contamination or loss of the can's protective seal. Reject the item and report it rather than opening or tasting the contents.
Which source is appropriate for ingredients used in a foodservice operation?|A supplier that meets applicable licensing and inspection requirements|A neighbor's uninspected home kitchen|An anonymous seller with no supplier records|Any source if the package looks clean|Food must come from an approved source that meets applicable requirements. Appearance alone cannot establish safe production, handling, or traceability.
# Personal Hygiene (9-16)
At least how long should the complete handwashing process take for a food handler?|20 seconds|5 seconds|10 seconds|60 seconds|Allow at least 20 seconds for the complete process, including wetting hands, applying soap, scrubbing, rinsing, and drying. Scrub hands and arms thoroughly for 10 to 15 seconds and dry with an approved method.
A food handler uses a personal phone during a break and then returns to assemble salads. What should happen before handling food?|Wash hands properly at the designated handwashing sink|Wipe hands on a clean apron|Put on gloves without washing hands|Rinse only the fingertips without soap|A phone can transfer contamination to the hands. Wash with soap and running water before returning to food preparation, then use clean utensils or fresh gloves as appropriate.
A food handler wearing single-use gloves finishes trimming raw chicken and must now prepare lettuce. What should the handler do first?|Remove the gloves, wash hands, and put on new gloves|Rinse the same gloves under running water|Wipe the gloves with a paper towel|Wear a second pair of gloves over the used pair|Change gloves when switching from raw poultry to ready-to-eat food. Hands must be washed before starting the new food task; single-use gloves must not be washed or reused.
Which practice helps prevent bare-hand contact when arranging ready-to-eat bread rolls for service?|Use clean tongs to handle the rolls|Handle the rolls with recently rinsed bare hands|Use a bare hand covered only by an apron|Pick up the rolls with bare hands after using hand antiseptic|Clean utensils such as tongs provide a barrier between hands and ready-to-eat food. Washing hands or applying hand antiseptic does not by itself authorize bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat food.
A food handler vomits before the start of a scheduled shift. What is the appropriate action?|Report the symptom to the person in charge before working with food|Work with food if disposable gloves are worn|Take medicine and return without telling anyone|Work only with ready-to-eat food until feeling better|Vomiting can signal an illness that spreads through food. Report the symptom promptly and do not begin food work; the person in charge must apply the employee health policy and applicable return-to-work requirements.
A food handler is permitted to work with a small infected cut on a finger. How must the wound be protected while handling food?|Cover it with an impermeable bandage or finger cot and a single-use glove|Cover it with a loose cloth bandage only|Rinse it at the start of the shift and leave it uncovered|Apply hand antiseptic instead of covering it|An infected hand or finger wound requires an impermeable cover, such as a suitable bandage or finger cot, with a single-use glove over it. Report the wound to the person in charge and follow the operation's health policy.
Which personal hygiene practice helps keep hair from contaminating exposed food?|Wear an effective hair restraint while preparing food|Brush hair over the preparation counter|Adjust hair frequently with gloved hands|Use an apron as the only hair restraint|An effective restraint, such as an appropriate hat or hairnet, keeps hair from contacting food and food-contact surfaces. Wash hands after touching hair and before resuming food preparation.
How should a food handler use an approved hand antiseptic?|As a supplement after proper handwashing, following its directions|As a replacement for washing visibly dirty hands|Instead of handwashing after using the restroom|Only on used gloves so they can be reused|Hand antiseptics do not replace washing with soap and running water. Use an approved product only as directed after proper handwashing, and allow it to dry before handling food or putting on gloves.
# Cross-contamination and Allergens (17-24)
Where should covered raw chicken be stored in a refrigerator that also holds ready-to-eat desserts?|Below the desserts in a container that prevents leaks|Above the desserts to keep the chicken colder|On the same tray as uncovered desserts|Anywhere, provided the chicken is labeled|Raw poultry juices can spread pathogens to food that will receive no further cooking. Keep raw chicken below ready-to-eat items and contain leaks; store other raw animal foods according to their required cooking temperatures.
A cutting board was used for raw beef and is needed for sliced tomatoes that will be served uncooked. What must happen before the board is reused?|Wash, rinse, sanitize, and air-dry the board|Wipe the board with a dry towel|Rinse the board briefly with cold water only|Turn the board over without cleaning it|Cleaning removes food residue, and sanitizing reduces pathogens on the cleaned surface. Complete both processes before switching from raw meat to ready-to-eat produce, or use a separate clean and sanitized board.
Which ingredient is one of the nine major food allergens recognized in the United States?|Sesame|Celery|Mustard|Garlic|Sesame is a U.S. major food allergen, along with milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, wheat, peanuts, and soybeans. Check ingredient information and follow the operation's procedures for preventing allergen cross-contact.
A guest reports a milk allergy and asks whether a sauce is safe. The server is unsure of its ingredients. What should the server do?|Tell the person in charge and verify the recipe and ingredient labels|Assume a white sauce is unsafe and any other color is safe|Taste the sauce to check for milk|Promise it is safe if the menu does not mention cheese|Never guess about an allergen. Communicate the allergy, check reliable ingredient and preparation information, and explain any uncertainty before the guest makes a decision.
A salad for a guest with a wheat allergy was accidentally topped with wheat croutons. What is the safest response?|Prepare a new salad with verified ingredients and clean equipment|Pick off the croutons and serve the same salad|Cover the croutons with extra dressing|Microwave the salad before removing the croutons|Removing visible croutons does not reliably remove wheat residue. Remake the order using ingredients, utensils, and preparation practices that prevent allergen cross-contact.
A pan contains traces of peanut sauce. Why is heating the pan alone insufficient before preparing a peanut-allergy order?|Cooking does not reliably eliminate food allergens|Food allergens disappear only when food is frozen|Allergens can be detected safely by taste|Peanut residue becomes harmless after it dries|Heat that kills many pathogens does not reliably remove or destroy allergenic proteins. Use properly cleaned equipment and utensils and follow the operation's allergen procedures.
Which practice is appropriate when scooping ice that will go into customers' drinks?|Use a designated scoop stored so its handle does not touch the ice|Use a drinking glass as the scoop|Pick up ice with a bare hand|Leave the scoop fully buried in the ice|Ice served in drinks is food and must be protected from contamination. Use a suitable scoop, keep hands off food-contact surfaces, and avoid glass because breakage can introduce fragments into the ice.
How should a food handler prepare a whole cucumber before slicing it for a ready-to-eat salad?|Wash it under running potable water before cutting|Wash it with dish detergent|Slice it first and wipe only the cut surfaces|Soak it in sanitizer intended only for equipment|Wash produce before cutting so the knife does not carry contamination from the outside to the inside. Use potable water and follow approved produce-handling procedures; dish detergent and equipment sanitizer are not suitable produce washes.
# Time and Temperature (25-32)
What is the maximum temperature for holding a TCS food such as prepared chicken salad under standard cold-holding control?|41°F (5°C)|45°F (7°C)|50°F (10°C)|70°F (21°C)|Hold cold TCS food at 41°F (5°C) or lower under standard cold-holding control. Use an appropriate thermometer and promptly report temperatures outside the required range.
What is the minimum temperature for holding cooked soup under standard hot-holding control?|135°F (57°C)|125°F (52°C)|130°F (54°C)|145°F (63°C)|Maintain hot-held TCS food at 135°F (57°C) or higher. Hot-holding equipment maintains the temperature of properly heated food; it should not be used to slowly reheat cold soup.
Which minimum internal cooking temperature is used for raw chicken under the standard ServSafe cooking guidance?|165°F (74°C), reached throughout the food|145°F (63°C) for 15 seconds|155°F (68°C) for 17 seconds|135°F (57°C) with no holding time|Poultry must reach 165°F (74°C) throughout; this standard does not require a further holding time. Check the thickest parts with a suitable thermometer rather than relying on the color of the meat or juices.
Which standard minimum internal cooking combination applies to a ground beef patty for a customer who has not requested undercooked food?|155°F (68°C) for 17 seconds|145°F (63°C) for 15 seconds|135°F (57°C) for 15 seconds|150°F (66°C) for 5 seconds|The standard ServSafe cooking combination for ground meat is 155°F (68°C) for 17 seconds. Grinding can distribute pathogens throughout the meat, so check the center with a suitable thermometer; approved equivalent combinations may also be used.
Which standard minimum internal cooking combination applies to a fish fillet served immediately?|145°F (63°C) for 15 seconds|135°F (57°C) for 15 seconds|140°F (60°C) for 5 seconds|155°F (68°C) for 5 seconds|The standard combination for fish is 145°F (63°C) for 15 seconds. Measure the internal temperature in the thickest part and follow any additional requirements for the specific product and operation.
Which method is appropriate for thawing frozen raw chicken before later preparation?|Thaw it in a refrigerator that keeps the food at 41°F (5°C) or lower|Leave it on a preparation counter overnight|Soak it in standing warm water for several hours|Place it beside a hot oven until the outside softens|Refrigerator thawing keeps the chicken under safe cold control while it thaws. Place it in a leakproof container below ready-to-eat foods; counter thawing can warm the outside into a range that supports pathogen growth.
Which cooling schedule meets the standard two-stage requirement for cooked TCS food?|135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F or lower within 6 total hours|135°F to 70°F within 4 hours, then to 41°F within 6 total hours|135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F within 8 total hours|135°F to 100°F within 2 hours, then to 41°F within 10 total hours|Cooked TCS food must cool from 135°F to 70°F within the first 2 hours and to 41°F or lower within a total of 6 hours. Use methods such as shallow pans, smaller portions, or an ice-water bath, and monitor both limits.
Where should a food handler place a cleaned and sanitized probe to check the temperature of a thick cooked food?|In the thickest part, away from bone and the pan|Against the outside of the cooking pan|Only on the surface of the food|Directly against a large bone|The probe must measure the food's internal temperature, not the pan, a bone, or the surrounding air. Follow the thermometer's instructions, allow the reading to stabilize, and check more than one location when needed.
# Cleaning and Sanitation (33-40)
What is the purpose of sanitizing a food-contact surface after it has been cleaned and rinsed?|Reduce pathogens on the surface to safe levels|Remove all visible food scraps before washing|Replace the need for detergent|Make the surface sterile for every future use|Cleaning removes food and soil; sanitizing then reduces pathogens to safe levels. Sanitizer works as intended only when the surface is properly cleaned and the correct concentration and contact time are used.
Which sequence is correct for manually washing utensils in a three-compartment sink?|Scrape, wash, rinse, sanitize, and air-dry|Scrape, sanitize, wash, rinse, and towel-dry|Wash, sanitize, scrape, rinse, and air-dry|Rinse, wash, towel-dry, and store without sanitizing|Remove food debris before washing, rinse away detergent, then sanitize using the required conditions. How should a food handler check the concentration of a chemical sanitizing solution?|Use a test kit or test strips designed for that sanitizer|Judge it only by the strength of its smell|Check only the color of the bucket|Assume the dispenser always provides the correct amount|Use the appropriate test kit or strips to measure the sanitizer concentration against the product's directions. A sanitizer label specifies a concentration range and a contact time. After utensils have been washed, rinsed, and sanitized, how should they be dried?|Allow them to air-dry on a clean, suitable rack|Wipe them with the towel used to clean the counter|Stack them while wet and seal them in a container|Dry them against a food handler's apron|Air-drying helps prevent recontamination and allows moisture to drain. Where should cleaning chemicals be stored when they are not in use?|In labeled containers in a designated area away from food and utensils|On a shelf directly above uncovered food|In unlabeled drink bottles near the preparation sink|On the same tray as clean serving utensils|Separate chemicals from food, equipment, utensils, linens, and single-use items to prevent contamination. Keep containers properly identified and follow the product's storage directions.
A food handler notices rodent droppings near a dry-storage shelf. What should happen?|Report the evidence promptly to the person in charge|Ignore it if the food packages are still sealed|Apply an unapproved pesticide beside the food|Move the droppings behind the shelf and continue working|Report pest evidence so the operation can protect affected food and arrange appropriate pest control. Pesticide application must follow applicable requirements and should be handled by an authorized pest-control professional.
Between uses, where should a reusable wet cloth used to wipe food preparation counters be kept?|In a properly maintained sanitizing solution of the required concentration|Draped over a food handler's shoulder|Inside a pocket next to a phone|On the edge of a handwashing sink|Store in-use wet wiping cloths in an appropriate sanitizer solution between uses and keep the solution at its required concentration. Keep cloths used around raw animal foods separate from cloths used on other surfaces.

# Providing Safe Food (1-9)
A manager introduces opening checks, observes food preparation, and corrects unsafe behavior during every shift. A hospital kitchen serves patients receiving chemotherapy. Which two bacterial growth conditions can a restaurant manager most directly control during routine food storage and service?|Time and temperature|The original source of a bacterium's genes and its shape|The age of the customer and the size of the dining room|The color and purchase price of the food|Limiting time at temperatures that support growth is a central control for TCS foods. A large batch of cooked rice is left warm for several hours before refrigeration. A restaurant wants to reduce the risk of deliberate food contamination. Who normally enforces the applicable restaurant food safety rules and conducts local establishment inspections?|The state or local regulatory authority with jurisdiction over the operation|The restaurant's advertising agency|Every food distributor independently|The manufacturer of the restaurant's ovens|Restaurant oversight is generally carried out by the relevant state or local regulatory authority. # Property Ownership and Land Use Controls and Regulations (1-23)
A seller and buyer disagree about whether a built-in cabinet is a fixture, and their contract is silent. A party's later preference does not alone determine whether an installed item became part of the real property.
A commercial tenant installs removable shelving for its business. Under the usual trade-fixture rule, what is the tenant generally entitled to do?|Remove qualifying trade fixtures within the permitted time and repair resulting damage|Leave the premises damaged without responsibility|Remove structural improvements regardless of their purpose|Treat all of the landlord's equipment as tenant property|Trade fixtures are installed by a tenant for its trade and may generally be removed subject to the lease and applicable timing rules. Removal does not excuse repairing damage or taking items that belong to the landlord.
Which estate ordinarily gives its owner the broadest ownership interest for an indefinite duration?|Fee simple absolute|An estate for years|A life estate measured by another person's life|A tenancy at sufferance|Fee simple absolute is the most complete private ownership estate, potentially lasting indefinitely. It remains subject to governmental powers and valid encumbrances; it does not mean ownership free of all regulation.
A life tenant deliberately destroys a valuable permanent improvement and substantially harms the future owner's interest. Which concept is most relevant?|Waste|Accretion|Novation|Subordination|A life tenant has a present right to use the property but must not commit waste that improperly injures the future interest. The life estate does not grant an unrestricted right to destroy the property's value.
An owner conveys a life estate to a friend and retains the interest that returns when the friend dies. What is the retained interest?|A reversion|An easement in gross|A leasehold assignment|A license to enter|A reversion is the future interest retained by a grantor when a lesser estate is conveyed. A remainder, by contrast, is a future interest created in someone other than the grantor.

What normally happens to the deceased owner's joint-tenancy interest?|It passes to the surviving joint tenant by right of survivorship|It must pass under the deceased owner's will|It automatically becomes the state's property|It converts into a separate leasehold estate|A valid joint tenancy includes a right of survivorship. Three investors want unequal ownership shares that each can leave to heirs. Which form of co-ownership ordinarily fits those objectives?|Tenancy in common|Joint tenancy with an inseverable survivorship requirement|A single life estate for all future generations|Tenancy at sufferance|Tenants in common may own unequal undivided interests, and there is no automatic right of survivorship. An owner's interest can generally pass through that owner's estate rather than automatically to the other co-owners.
Under California's general community-property rule, which acquisition is ordinarily separate property rather than community property?|A gift made solely to one spouse|Wages earned by either spouse during marriage while domiciled in California|Property purchased solely with ordinary community earnings|Savings accumulated solely from community wages|Property acquired by one spouse by gift or inheritance is generally separate property, as is property owned before marriage. Agreements, tracing, commingling, and other facts can affect a particular ownership analysis.
A recorded driveway easement benefits one parcel and burdens the neighboring parcel. The right is attached to the benefited land. What type of easement is this?|An easement appurtenant|An easement in gross benefiting only a utility company|A possessory fee estate in the neighbor's entire parcel|A temporary permission that necessarily ends on every sale|An easement appurtenant benefits a dominant parcel and burdens a servient parcel. When valid and not otherwise limited or terminated, the right generally transfers with the benefited land.
A utility holds an easement for its transmission lines that benefits the utility rather than a neighboring dominant parcel. How is the easement generally classified?|An easement in gross|A tenancy in common|A remainder estate|A residential leasehold|An easement in gross benefits a person or entity rather than a particular dominant parcel. Utility easements are common examples; their scope and transferability depend on the governing law and instrument.
Parcel A has the right to use a path across Parcel B. In this easement relationship, which parcel is the servient tenement?|Parcel B, which is burdened by the path|Parcel A, which benefits from the path|Both parcels solely because they have different owners|Neither parcel because an easement conveys full ownership|The servient tenement bears the burden of an easement. The dominant tenement receives its benefit; an easement grants a specified use rather than fee ownership of the burdened land.
A buyer suspects that a neighbor's garage extends across the boundary. A recorded declaration restricts exterior paint colors in a common interest development. A city limits building height and permitted land uses through zoning. A public agency acquires private land for a public road through a lawful compulsory acquisition. A person dies owning property, with no valid disposition and no legal heirs entitled to inherit. Which governmental right may eventually apply?|Escheat|Accretion|Hypothecation|Substitution|Escheat concerns property passing to the state when no legally entitled private successor exists. It is different from taxation, police power, or condemnation for a public project.
An assessment is described as an ad valorem property tax. What does ad valorem mean?|Based on the property's value|Charged only when a property produces rent|Always the same dollar amount for every parcel|A voluntary payment to a homeowners association|Ad valorem means according to value. The applicable assessed value and tax rules determine the charge; special assessments or special taxes can operate differently.
A parcel has unusual physical limitations, and its owner seeks relief from a dimensional zoning requirement without changing the zoning district. # Laws of Agency and Fiduciary Duties (24-48)
In a real estate agency relationship, who is the principal?|The client for whom the authorized agent acts|Every person who views the property|Only the county recorder|The escrow company's bank|The principal authorizes an agent to act on the principal's behalf within the agency's scope. A customer or other transaction participant is not automatically that agent's client.
A broker is authorized to market and negotiate the sale of one property for an owner. This limited transaction authority is commonly an example of what?|Special agency|Unlimited authority over all of the owner's affairs|A transfer of the owner's fee title to the broker|A court-appointed probate administration|A special agent is authorized for a particular act or transaction. A general agent may have broader ongoing authority within a business or activity, such as an agreed property-management role.
A seller and broker sign a listing agreement that states the broker's services and authority. How was this agency most clearly created?|By an express agreement|By escheat|By a court foreclosure sale|By a unilateral change to the zoning map|An express agency is created through stated agreement rather than merely inferred conduct. Separate writing and enforceability requirements apply to real estate employment and compensation agreements.
An owner learns all material facts about an agent's unauthorized transaction and voluntarily adopts the transaction. Which agency concept may apply?|Ratification|Accession|Amortization|Subrogation of a title insurer|Ratification can occur when a principal knowingly affirms an unauthorized act, subject to applicable requirements. It does not mean that any unauthorized act automatically binds an uninformed principal.
A principal's conduct reasonably leads a third party to believe someone has authority to act for the principal. Which concept is most relevant?|Ostensible authority|Authority created solely by the supposed agent's unsupported claim|Adverse possession|A private deed restriction|Ostensible authority depends on the principal's conduct and the third party's reasonable reliance. A person cannot ordinarily create such authority merely by asserting that they represent someone else.
A listing agent can earn a secret personal benefit by recommending an inferior offer. Which fiduciary obligation is most directly threatened?|Loyalty to the principal|The county's power of taxation|The buyer's right to depreciate appliances|The lender's right to change an interest-rate index|An agent must put the principal's interests ahead of undisclosed personal gain. Conflicts, compensation, and material personal interests require appropriate disclosure and consent rather than secret profit.
A buyer privately tells the buyer's agent the maximum price the buyer is willing to pay. May the agent freely share it with the seller to speed negotiations?|No; the agent must protect confidential client information unless disclosure is authorized or legally required|Yes, because all negotiation information is public|Yes, whenever the seller asks politely|Yes, because confidentiality ends when an offer is drafted|Confidential financial and negotiating information is part of the agent's fiduciary responsibility. The agent should not disclose the buyer's ceiling without appropriate authorization or a legal duty to disclose.
A seller orders the listing agent to hide a known recurring roof leak from prospective buyers. What should the agent do?|Refuse to conceal the material defect and comply with disclosure duties|Obey because loyalty requires every instruction to be followed|Disclose only if a buyer discovers the leak first|State that an as-is clause eliminates all disclosure duties|An agent's duty of obedience extends to lawful instructions, not fraud or concealment. Known material facts affecting value or desirability must be handled in accordance with applicable disclosure obligations.
A brokerage proposes representing both buyer and seller in the same sale. What is essential for lawful disclosed dual agency?|The informed consent of both principals and compliance with required disclosures|Consent from only the party paying the larger commission|Silence from both parties after closing|A clause stating that the brokerage owes no duties to anyone|Dual agency creates conflicts and requires appropriate disclosure and both principals' consent. It does not eliminate fiduciary duties or permit unrestricted sharing of each client's confidential information.
In a California dual agency, the seller privately states a lower price the seller would accept. What should the dual agent do without express permission to disclose it?|Keep that confidential negotiating information from the buyer|Automatically disclose it because the agent represents both sides|Publish it in the property advertisement|Treat it as public information once the listing price is posted|California limits a dual agent's disclosure of confidential information such as a seller's willingness to accept less or a buyer's willingness to pay more without the relevant client's express permission. Dual agency does not authorize using one client's confidential position against that client.
A licensee is selling a property owned by an LLC in which the licensee has a financial interest. What is the appropriate practice?|Disclose the licensee's relevant ownership interest and licensed status as required|Conceal the interest because title is in the LLC's name|Disclose only after the buyer waives inspections|Assume an LLC always removes every conflict of interest|Acting as a principal or having a financial interest can create material conflicts. A licensee must make required disclosures and avoid misleading the other party merely because an entity holds legal title.
A salesperson negotiates a transaction while affiliated with an employing broker. Which statement best describes the salesperson's professional role?|The salesperson performs licensed activities through the employing broker's authority and supervision|The salesperson automatically operates an independent brokerage|The salesperson may accept any outside commission without broker involvement|The salesperson's client duties disappear because a broker is employed|A California salesperson acts under the responsible broker rather than independently conducting a brokerage business. The broker's supervisory role does not excuse the salesperson from applicable duties and lawful conduct.
A broker receives a buyer's earnest-money check. Which fiduciary responsibility is most directly involved in accurately tracking and safeguarding it?|Accounting for entrusted funds|Guaranteeing the buyer's investment return|Determining the county's tax rate|Issuing a title insurance policy|An agent must properly handle and account for money and property entrusted by others. Accurate records, timely lawful disposition, and avoiding conversion or improper commingling are fundamental obligations.
A salesperson is uncertain whether a claimed addition was properly permitted. Which response best reflects reasonable care?|Identify the uncertainty, investigate appropriate sources, and recommend qualified verification|Assure the buyer it was permitted because it looks professionally built|Repeat the seller's claim as a verified fact without checking|Promise that a title policy guarantees building-code compliance|A licensee should distinguish verified facts from assumptions and make appropriate inquiries. Material uncertainty about permits should be disclosed and investigated rather than resolved through an unsupported assurance.
A listing agent receives another offer while one offer is being negotiated. What is the appropriate general practice, absent a lawful contrary instruction or agreement?|Promptly present the offer to the seller|Reject it without telling the seller|Hide it to protect the agent's preferred buyer|Wait until closing before revealing it|Diligence and loyalty generally require presenting offers promptly so the principal can decide. The agent must follow lawful instructions and applicable agreements rather than substituting a personal preference for the client's decision.
A buyer asks what commission rate California law requires for a residential sale. Which answer is correct?|Real estate compensation is negotiable and is not fixed by law|Every residential sale requires a 6% commission|DRE sets a minimum commission for each county|The highest local brokerage rate is legally mandatory|Broker compensation is negotiated through the applicable agreements. Neither state law nor a trade association establishes a mandatory commission rate for ordinary real estate transactions.
Competing independent brokers agree that none will accept listings below a particular commission rate. What concern does this raise?|Unlawful price fixing under antitrust principles|A required DRE fee schedule|A valid public zoning regulation|An automatic exemption from competition law|Competitors must not agree to fix compensation or minimum fees. Each brokerage makes its own lawful pricing decisions, and consumers may negotiate compensation and services.
Which terms must a California buyer-broker representation agreement address?|Compensation, services, when compensation is due, and termination|Only the buyer's favorite neighborhood|Only the seller's mortgage balance|Only an oral promise to negotiate compensation later|Civil Code section 1670.50 requires specified buyer-representation terms. A written agreement clarifies the services and payment obligations; it should not leave required terms to an unsupported later assumption.
Under California's adopted buyer-representation regulations, what is the general rebuttable presumption about practicable signing time for a buyer's agent?|It is practicable to obtain the signed agreement before an in-person or qualifying virtual showing|Signing is never needed before escrow closes|An agreement is required only if the buyer requests one|Only a trade association, never California law, addresses signing time|Section 2906.2 creates a rebuttable presumption concerning signing before the buyer's agent shows property in person or through the defined virtual showing. The statute also requires execution as soon as practicable and no later than the buyer's offer; a seller-only open-house agent is treated differently.
For an ordinary California buyer-broker agreement with an individual buyer, how do the adopted regulations define the statutory maximum three-month term?|90 calendar days, subject to the regulation's counting rules|120 business days|An unlimited term if the agreement is exclusive|One calendar year in every transaction|Section 2906.1 defines three months as 90 calendar days and specifies how the period is counted. The statutory initial-term limitation has an entity-buyer exception for a corporation, LLC, or partnership, so it should not be generalized without checking the parties.
An individual buyer's California representation agreement is about to expire. Which renewal procedure is appropriate under the adopted rules?|A written, dated renewal signed by the parties before expiration, within the applicable term limit|Automatic renewal for another year unless the buyer objects|An oral extension made only by the agent|Changing the expiration date after the buyer has signed without authorization|Buyer-broker representation agreements do not renew automatically. Renewals must meet the statute's writing and signature requirements and the adopted regulation's timing and term rules.
Before an agent and buyer execute a California buyer-broker representation agreement, what must the agent provide under Civil Code section 1670.50?|The required disclosure regarding real estate agency relationships|A guaranteed appraisal result|The seller's confidential minimum price|A promise that compensation cannot be negotiated|The statute requires the agency disclosure form before execution of the buyer-broker agreement. Disclosing agency duties is distinct from obtaining consent to any particular representation arrangement or agreeing to compensation.
A seller's agent does not represent the buyer. What duty still applies in dealings with that buyer?|Honesty, fair dealing, and required disclosure of material facts|A right to make knowingly false statements to advance the seller's interests|A duty to reveal every confidential seller negotiating limit|No duty at all because the buyer has a different agent|An agent owes fiduciary duties to the principal and additional duties to other transaction participants. Representing one side does not permit misrepresentation or concealment of information that must be disclosed.
A listing expires without a sale. Which statement about information learned during the agency is most accurate?|Termination does not automatically authorize disclosure of the former client's confidential information|All private negotiating information becomes public the next morning|The broker may sell the former client's financial records without consent|Expiration transfers the owner's title to the broker|The agency's termination does not automatically release the agent from duties concerning confidential information. Record retention and other obligations can also survive the end of the relationship.
An ordinary personal agency is not coupled with an interest. What generally happens when the individual principal dies?|The agent's ordinary authority terminates, subject to applicable law|The agent automatically acquires the principal's real property|The agency becomes a perpetual listing binding every heir|The agent becomes the estate's court-appointed representative without any appointment|Death generally terminates an ordinary personal agency, unlike certain powers coupled with an interest. Handling the deceased person's estate or completing a transfer may require authority from an authorized estate representative or court process.
# Property Valuation and Financial Analysis (49-69)
Which description best expresses market value rather than merely a transaction's sale price?|The most probable price under the defined conditions of an informed, open, and competitive market|Any amount a seller writes in an advertisement|The original construction cost in every case|The highest price ever paid for any home in the county|Market value is an opinion developed under specified market assumptions, not simply an asking price or a single sale price. Unusual financing, duress, or nonmarket motivations can affect a particular transaction's price.
A buyer will not pay substantially more for one property than for an equally desirable available substitute. Which valuation principle is illustrated?|Substitution|Escheat|Novation|Accession by inheritance|Substitution links value to the cost of acquiring a suitable alternative with comparable utility. It underlies comparisons of competing properties and helps explain why excessive improvements may not recover their cost.
Which set of tests is normally used to analyze a property's highest and best use?|Legally permissible, physically possible, financially feasible, and maximally productive|Most expensive, newest, largest, and most fashionable|Any use preferred by the current owner|Any use that ignores zoning and physical limitations|Highest and best use must satisfy all four tests. A proposed use that would be profitable but is legally prohibited or physically impossible cannot be accepted without resolving that limitation.
A swimming pool costs $60,000 to install, but credible market evidence shows it adds $20,000 to the property's value. What is its indicated contribution to value?|$20,000|$60,000|$40,000|$80,000|The principle of contribution measures an improvement's effect on the value of the whole property. Its construction cost is not automatically equal to the amount buyers will pay for it.
A modest home benefits in value from being surrounded by substantially more valuable, compatible homes. Which appraisal principle is most directly illustrated?|Progression|Regression|Escheat|Acceleration|Progression describes the positive effect of association with superior neighboring properties. Compatibility, market preferences, and other location factors still influence the actual amount of any benefit.
A very expensive home loses some market support because surrounding properties are substantially inferior. Which principle is most directly illustrated?|Regression|Progression|A statutory tax exemption|An automatic increase in replacement cost|Regression describes the adverse effect that lower-value surroundings can have on a superior property. The most expensive improvement in an area may not obtain a price equal to its cost.
A comparable sold for $640,000 without a pool. The subject has a pool worth $20,000 more in the market, and all other features are equal. How should the comparable be adjusted?|Add $20,000, producing an indicated subject value of $660,000|Subtract $20,000, producing $620,000|Add the pool's original cost regardless of value|Make no adjustment because only the subject has a pool|In the sales comparison approach, adjustments are made to the comparable to reflect differences from the subject. An inferior comparable receives an upward adjustment for the missing value-contributing feature.
A comparable sold for $680,000 and has a superior garage worth $20,000 more than the subject's garage. Everything else is equal. What is the adjusted comparable price?|$660,000|$700,000|$680,000|$640,000|A superior comparable is adjusted downward to make it equivalent to the subject. Subtract the $20,000 market difference from the comparable's $680,000 sale price.
A comparable sold a year ago for $600,000. Supported market data indicate a 5% increase since that sale, with no other differences. What time-adjusted price is indicated?|$630,000|$570,000|$605,000|$650,000|Apply the supported change to the comparable's sale price: $600,000 multiplied by 1.05 equals $630,000. Time adjustments require evidence of market conditions rather than an unsupported assumption.
Under a basic cost approach, which calculation indicates property value?|Land value plus improvement replacement cost less accrued depreciation|Land value minus all improvement costs|Gross rent multiplied by the loan interest rate|The owner's original down payment plus unpaid taxes|The cost approach estimates land value separately and adds the depreciated value of improvements. Land is not depreciated in the same way as buildings in this appraisal calculation.
An appraiser identifies a worn roof and deteriorated exterior paint. Which type of accrued depreciation is most directly involved?|Physical deterioration|External economic obsolescence only|A change in zoning classification|A mortgage prepayment penalty|Wear, damage, and aging of physical components are physical deterioration. Whether an item is economically curable depends on the cost to correct it compared with the resulting benefit.
A home's outdated floor plan has a bedroom accessible only through another bedroom. What type of depreciation may this represent?|Functional obsolescence|Physical deterioration of the land itself|A title insurance exception|An increase in loan amortization|Functional obsolescence concerns a property's design, layout, or utility relative to market expectations. A component can be physically sound but still suffer a loss in value from an inferior design.
A well-maintained house loses value because of newly increased noise from a nearby industrial operation. What type of depreciation is most relevant?|External obsolescence|Normal wear of the house's roof|An internal accounting error|A reduction in the property's legal lot size by agreement|External obsolescence results from influences outside the property, such as adverse nearby uses or economic changes. It differs from defects in the building's condition or internal layout.
An income property has stabilized annual net operating income of $96,000 and an appropriate capitalization rate of 8%. What value does direct capitalization indicate?|$1,200,000|$768,000|$960,000|$12,000,000|Under direct capitalization, value equals NOI divided by the capitalization rate. $96,000 divided by 0.08 equals $1,200,000; the selected rate must be supported by the relevant market and assumptions.
A property has $120,000 annual potential gross income, $6,000 vacancy and collection loss, and $34,000 operating expenses. Subtracting $34,000 in operating expenses gives $80,000 NOI; debt service, income taxes, and depreciation are not operating expenses in this calculation.
An investment property has annual NOI of $75,000 and a value of $1,250,000. What capitalization rate is indicated?|6%|16.67%|9.375%|0.6%|The capitalization rate equals NOI divided by value. $75,000 divided by $1,250,000 equals 0.06, or 6%; this is not automatically the investor's cash-on-cash return.
A rental house sells for $600,000 and has monthly gross rent of $3,000. What monthly gross rent multiplier is indicated?|200|20|16.67|2,000|A monthly GRM equals sale price divided by monthly gross rent. $600,000 divided by $3,000 equals 200; do not combine annual rent with a multiplier calculated from monthly rent.
A property sells for $720,000 and produces annual gross income of $60,000. What annual gross income multiplier is indicated?|12|120|8.33|1.2|The annual gross income multiplier is $720,000 divided by $60,000, or 12. A gross multiplier does not directly deduct operating expenses or account for financing differences.
An investor receives $18,000 annual before-tax cash flow after debt service on a $200,000 cash investment. What is the cash-on-cash return?|9%|11.11%|90%|0.9%|Cash-on-cash return compares the stated cash flow with the actual cash invested. $18,000 divided by $200,000 equals 9%; it is different from a capitalization rate based on NOI and total property value.
Using 43,560 square feet per acre, how many square feet are in a 0.75-acre parcel?|32,670 square feet|58,080 square feet|30,000 square feet|34,848 square feet|Multiply the acreage by 43,560: 0.75 multiplied by 43,560 equals 32,670 square feet. The conversion describes area and does not establish the parcel's shape or legal boundaries.

# Financing (70-83)

In a typical deed-of-trust arrangement, which party is the trustor?|The borrower who conveys the security interest|The lender receiving the beneficial security interest|The county tax assessor|The title insurer in every transaction|The borrower is the trustor, the lender is the beneficiary, and the trustee holds the authority provided by the trust deed and applicable law. A buyer obtains a $480,000 loan on a property valued at $600,000 for this calculation. Two points on $400,000 equal 0.02 multiplied by $400,000, or $8,000, not a percentage of the property's sale price unless the amounts happen to be the same.

In a standard fully amortizing fixed-rate loan with level principal-and-interest payments, what usually happens over time?|The interest portion decreases and the principal portion increases|The total principal-and-interest payment doubles every year|The principal balance never decreases|All payments are applied only to principal|As the unpaid principal declines, less interest is due for each payment period, leaving more of the same scheduled payment for principal. What is occurring?|Negative amortization|Full amortization without interest|A deed reconveyance|An automatic reduction in the interest rate|Negative amortization increases the loan balance because the payment does not cover accrued interest. # 1-15: Life policy types and annuities (15).
A parent wants a level death benefit to replace income only during the next 20 years and does not need cash value. Which policy most directly matches that stated need?|20-year level term life|Single-premium whole life|An immediate life annuity|A hospital indemnity policy|Level term insurance provides a stated death benefit for a specified term and generally has no cash value. The coverage period can be matched to a temporary income-replacement need.
A renewable term policy reaches the end of its initial term while the insured's health has deteriorated. What does the renewal privilege generally allow within the policy's limits?|Continued term coverage without new evidence of insurability, usually at a higher age-based premium|Permanent coverage with no further premiums|A refund of every premium regardless of policy terms|Renewal at the original premium for every future age|Renewability protects the ability to continue coverage within the contract's renewal limits without proving insurability again. It does not ordinarily freeze the premium as the insured grows older.
An insured exercises a term policy's conversion privilege before its deadline. What is the principal advantage of conversion?|Obtaining an eligible permanent policy without new evidence of insurability|Automatically doubling the death benefit without cost|Receiving the term policy's nonexistent cash value|Converting the policy into Medicare coverage|Conversion generally permits an eligible permanent policy without new medical underwriting when the contract's conditions are satisfied. Premiums and available products follow the conversion terms rather than the original term premium.
A borrower wants a life insurance death benefit that broadly follows a declining mortgage balance. Which design is most closely aligned with that goal?|Decreasing term life|Increasing term life|A straight life annuity|A level hospital cash benefit|Decreasing term coverage reduces the scheduled death benefit over time. It can match a declining debt, although the policy's schedule and the actual loan balance should be compared.
A whole life policy is described as 20-pay life. What does the number 20 ordinarily describe?|The scheduled premium-paying period, while coverage can continue for life|The maximum number of years the insured may live|The period after which all cash value must disappear|The number of beneficiaries who must be named|Limited-pay whole life concentrates the required premiums into a shorter scheduled period. If the required premiums are paid and the contract stays in force, the lifetime coverage continues beyond that period.
A universal life owner reduces premium payments for several years while monthly policy charges continue. Which risk should the agent explain?|The policy can lapse if available value and required payments are insufficient to cover charges|Flexible premiums eliminate all cost-of-insurance deductions|The insurer must fund every shortfall forever without conditions|The policy automatically becomes fully paid-up term insurance|Universal life combines flexible premium arrangements with continuing policy charges. Insufficient funding can exhaust policy value and cause lapse, subject to any applicable guarantees and their funding conditions.
A variable life policy allocates cash value to equity subaccounts. Who generally bears the investment risk of those subaccount results?|The policyowner|The Texas Department of Insurance|The named beneficiary's employer|The state guaranty association as a promised investment return|Variable life cash values depend on selected separate-account investments, so the owner bears their investment risk. Contractual death-benefit guarantees, if any, must be distinguished from the variable cash value.
An indexed universal life illustration uses an index-crediting formula. Which statement about that formula is accurate?|Caps, participation rates, floors, and policy charges can affect actual policy results|The owner directly owns every stock in the referenced index|The illustration guarantees the index's full future return after all charges|A crediting floor always prevents policy value from declining because of charges|Indexed crediting follows the contract's formula rather than direct stock ownership. A floor on credited interest does not eliminate policy charges or guarantee that overall cash value cannot fall.
A customer pays a single premium and wants contractual income payments to begin next month. Which annuity timing best fits the request?|An immediate annuity|A deferred annuity with a 20-year accumulation period|A decreasing term policy|A disability income policy|An immediate annuity begins income within a short period after purchase, generally within one year. A deferred annuity postpones the income phase and can have a substantial accumulation period.
Under a traditional fixed annuity's general-account guarantee, who bears the investment risk of earning enough to meet the promised minimum interest crediting?|The insurer|The annuitant's physician|The owner's contingent beneficiary|The producer personally through a brokerage account|The insurer manages general-account assets and is responsible for the fixed contract's guarantees, subject to its claims-paying ability. This differs from a variable annuity's investment performance risk borne by the owner.
A fixed indexed annuity credits 80% of a positive index change, subject to a 6% annual cap. The index rises 10%, and no spread or other adjustment applies. What interest rate is credited for that year?|6%|8%|10%|0%|First apply the participation rate: 10% multiplied by 80% is 8%. The 6% cap then limits the credited rate to 6%, under the simplified formula stated in the question.
A retiree chooses a life-only annuity without a refund or period-certain feature. Two spouses want annuity income to continue while either spouse remains alive. Which payout form addresses that objective?|Joint-and-survivor life income|Single-life income on only one spouse with no guarantee|A five-year period-certain payout with no life contingency|A lump-sum surrender|A joint-and-survivor annuity continues a specified benefit to the survivor after the first annuitant dies. The selected survivor percentage and other guarantees determine how much continues.
Which life insurance arrangement ordinarily pays its death benefit only after both covered spouses have died?|Survivorship, or second-to-die, life insurance|Joint first-to-die life insurance|An individual accidental injury policy|A single-life immediate annuity|Survivorship life pays following the second covered death. Joint first-to-die life ordinarily pays at the first covered death, so the timing of the insurance need matters.
A return-of-premium term policy promises a refund if the insured survives the term and satisfies the stated conditions. What should an agent explain?|The refund is governed by the contract and does not mean ordinary term insurance always builds cash value|Every term policy must refund premiums with stock-market interest|The insured can stop premiums immediately and still receive the full promised refund|The refund replaces the need for a death benefit during the term|Return-of-premium term adds a conditional refund feature to term coverage, usually at a higher premium. It should not be described as a feature of every term policy or as an unrestricted investment guarantee.

The child's policy remains subject to the rider's age and other conditions.

A life policy names a spouse as primary beneficiary and an adult daughter as contingent beneficiary. The spouse dies before the insured, and no changes are made. Who ordinarily receives the proceeds if the daughter survives the insured?|The daughter as contingent beneficiary|The deceased spouse's employer automatically|The producer who sold the policy|The insurer's shareholders regardless of the designation|A contingent beneficiary generally receives proceeds when no primary beneficiary survives the insured, subject to the policy and applicable law. An owner has named an irrevocable beneficiary. What restriction should the owner generally expect?|The beneficiary's consent is generally required for changes that impair that beneficiary's rights|The owner may always replace that beneficiary without consent|The beneficiary has no enforceable interest until the insurer is liquidated|The producer may change the designation for convenience|An irrevocable designation grants protected rights that a revocable designation does not. Changes affecting those rights, including a beneficiary change, generally require the irrevocable beneficiary's consent.
A life policy has a $250,000 death benefit and $18,000 of outstanding policy loan principal and interest at death. With no other adjustments, how much is payable to the beneficiary?|$232,000|$250,000|$268,000|$18,000|The outstanding loan balance and accrued loan interest reduce the death proceeds: $250,000 minus $18,000 equals $232,000. Policy borrowing can also increase lapse risk if not managed.
An owner stops paying whole life premiums and chooses reduced paid-up insurance. What is the usual result?|A smaller permanent death benefit purchased with the available nonforfeiture value, with no further scheduled premiums|The original death benefit continues as term insurance for a limited time|The cash surrender value is paid immediately and all insurance ends|The original death benefit doubles for life without charge|Reduced paid-up insurance uses available value to provide a lower amount of permanent coverage. Extended term instead generally uses available value to continue the original face amount for a limited period.
An agent illustrates future dividends on a participating whole life policy. Which disclosure is appropriate?|Dividends are not guaranteed and can differ from the illustration|The illustration makes every future dividend contractually guaranteed|Dividends are the same as interest guaranteed by the U.S. Treasury|All participating policies must pay identical dividends|Participating policy dividends reflect the insurer's experience and declared distribution. They should be clearly separated from guaranteed policy values and never presented as certain future payments.
A whole life policy has an elected automatic premium loan provision and sufficient available loan value. What can happen when a premium is not paid by the end of the grace period?|A policy loan can pay the premium and accrue interest|The unpaid premium is permanently forgiven without borrowing|The policy must immediately pay the full death benefit|The insurer must replace the coverage with Medicare|An automatic premium loan uses available loan value to fund an overdue premium under the policy's terms. It creates debt and interest and does not provide an unlimited guarantee against lapse.
Which documents ordinarily form the entire life insurance contract when the application is attached to the policy?|The policy, attached application, and incorporated riders or endorsements|Only the agent's oral sales promise|An advertisement that was never incorporated into the policy|A beneficiary's unrelated tax return|The entire-contract provision identifies the documents that make up the agreement. Oral statements do not independently rewrite the written policy, application, and incorporated amendments.
A life policy contains a clearly applicable exclusion for a specified aviation activity. Why is explaining that exclusion at delivery important?|A death connected to the excluded activity may not receive the otherwise expected benefit|Every aviation-related death must be covered despite the exclusion|An exclusion guarantees a higher cash surrender value|Explaining an exclusion gives the agent authority to erase it orally|An exclusion narrows the coverage promised by the policy. The agent should explain its scope accurately and avoid implying that an oral assurance can override the insurer's written contract.
A policy's misstatement-of-age clause adjusts benefits to what the paid premium would have purchased at the correct age. What is the adjusted benefit?|$100,000|$60,000|$600,000|$1,000,000|The premium buys 100 units because $600 divided by $6 equals 100. A beneficiary wants life insurance proceeds distributed over exactly ten years rather than for a lifetime. A life income option is instead tied to the covered recipient's lifetime and may not end on a predetermined date.
An owner asks to reinstate a lapsed life policy rather than apply for a new one. What should the agent explain?|Reinstatement can require evidence of insurability, overdue premiums, and interest under the existing contract's conditions|Reinstatement always requires no payment and no underwriting|Every lapsed contract remains reinstatable forever|Reinstatement automatically creates a larger face amount at no additional cost|Reinstatement restores an eligible lapsed policy when its requirements are met. The available period and required evidence, premium payments, interest, and loan treatment are governed by the policy and applicable law.

An insurer takes adverse action based on a consumer report.
